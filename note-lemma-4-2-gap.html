A congestion‑counting gap in Lemma 4.2

On “A tight meta-theorem for LOCAL certification of MSO2 properties within bounded treewidth graphs”

Linda Cook, Eun Jung Kim, Tomáš Masařík — arXiv:2503.19671

Posted — reviewed with one of the authors prior to publication

Lemma 4.2 builds a set of one-way paths that stand in for an elimination tree, using a k-colorable guidance system to limit how many paths can pass through any one vertex. The step that counts those paths has a gap, though: it assumes something about the paths' endpoints that the construction doesn't actually guarantee.

The setup

Take a vertex v in the elimination tree F (not the root), whose parent p(v) is not actually next to it in G. The proof builds a path Pv to stand in for that missing connection. Both v and p(v) live inside the same "adhesion" at some tree node t, and the guidance system gives us one vertex x that "reaches" both of them, meaning v and p(v) are each the root of an in-tree that contains x. The path Pv is built from two pieces glued together at x:

  • from x back to v, along the in-tree rooted at v (reversed);
  • from x onward to p(v), along the in-tree rooted at p(v).

Each path is really two different in-trees stuck together at x.

The claimed bound

To show that not too many paths pass through some vertex y, the proof looks at the set S = {s1,…,sℓ} of all in-tree roots whose in-tree passes through y. Since Λ is k-colorable, any group of in-trees that all overlap each other has at most k members, so ℓ ≤ k. The proof then says:

flagged step

“…we add a new oriented path to P connecting one vertex in {s1,…,sℓ} to another vertex in {s1,…,sℓ} only when the latter vertex is the parent of the former vertex in F.”

Proof of Lemma 4.2, p. 15

The idea is that every path through y connects two members of S, the way an edge connects two points in a tree drawn on S. Since a tree on ℓ points has at most ℓ − 1 edges, the proof concludes there are at most k − 1 such paths.

Where it breaks

That "edges of a tree on S" picture only works if both ends of every path through y are actually in S. Remember, though, that Pv is glued from two separate in-trees at x, so for y to lie on Pv it only has to sit on one of those two pieces, not both. Say y sits on the piece going from x to p(v): that puts p(v) in S, but nothing says v is in S too. The in-tree rooted at v might never even pass near y. The same problem happens the other way round, and the only time both roots are guaranteed to be pinned down together is the special case y = x.

The paths going through y don't line up neatly with pairs of points in S, then. They're more like loose ends hanging off of S, and nothing in the coloring argument stops there from being a lot of them.

A concrete overload

Here's a case where it actually breaks. Suppose vertex u has many children in F, call them v1, …, vm, and all of them happen to route through the same in-tree rooted at u (nothing in the construction rules this out; Λ doesn't have to give a different in-tree for each root). Pick a vertex y sitting on the shared trunk of that in-tree, somewhere below u but above every "reaching" vertex xi. Now y lies inside every single path Pvi, so as many as m paths pass through it, and because this only involves one in-tree, k-colorability sees no conflict at all and never limits m. A vertex of F can have any number of children, so m can be much bigger than k, which breaks the claimed bound of k − 1.

Scope of the gap

This gap doesn't stay inside Lemma 4.2. Lemma 4.3 has its own way of counting, and that counting looks fine on its own, but Lemma 4.3 doesn't start from scratch: for each marginal graph G⋆t it needs an elimination tree and a path system with congestion at most some number k1, and in the proof of Proposition 3.7 that input is built by calling Lemma 4.2. So Lemma 4.3's result depends on Lemma 4.2's congestion bound being correct.

Lemma 4.2's congestion bound is exactly what's broken, though, so k1 is never actually proven, and neither is Lemma 4.3's own output, 3k1·k22, even though the steps inside Lemma 4.3 itself are fine. That broken number is the bound f(ω) that Proposition 3.7 hands off, and it gets used directly in the proof of Theorem 1.1. The gap isn't contained to one lemma's writeup; it reaches Lemma 4.3 and Proposition 3.7 on its way into the paper's main theorem.

What this does and doesn't affect

As written, the proof of Theorem 1.1 has a real gap in it, not a small, contained slip in one auxiliary lemma. I don't think the theorem itself is false. A fixed counting argument, say limiting branching inside each in-tree, or counting congestion per in-tree vertex instead of per root, would probably still give some bound in terms of k, and that bound would carry back through Lemma 4.3 and Proposition 3.7 to Theorem 1.1. But "probably fixable" isn't the same as proven, and right now this step of the proof isn't proven.

Status. Raised with one of the authors, who acknowledged the gap in the write-up of Lemma 4.2. This note records it for anyone else reading the paper closely; check back or contact the authors for the current state of the writeup.
